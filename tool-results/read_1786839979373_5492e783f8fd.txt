   214→   214→**2. The Strategic Pivot**
   215→   215→
   216→   216→The existing blueprint positions DenialDefender as \"the agent that
   217→   217→fights your insurance company for you\" and, operationally, as \"AI that
   218→   218→writes insurance appeal letters.\" That framing is emotionally resonant
   219→   219→but technically dangerous: it invites the judge to evaluate the project
   220→   220→as a letter generator, in which case a single hallucinated citation or
   221→   221→an unverified claim collapses the entire submission. The pivot is to
   222→   222→re-position the product one level up the abstraction stack, from output
   223→   223→to workflow.
   224→   224→
   225→   225→The new target identity is evidence-grounded, human-governed autonomous
   226→   226→denial-appeal operations. The central loop is no longer \"upload denial
   227→   227→→ produce letter\" but a ten-stage closed loop in which the letter is
   228→   228→merely the most visible intermediate artifact and the outcome is the
   229→   229→thing the system actually optimizes for:
   230→   230→
   231→   231→*Figure 2.1 --- The ten-stage closed loop. The appeal letter is an
   232→   232→intermediate artifact, not the deliverable; the deliverable is a
   233→   233→workflow that measurably improves.*
   234→   234→
   235→   235→> REAL PUBLIC HEALTHCARE EVIDENCE\
   236→   236→> v\
   237→   237→> SYNTHETIC CASE\
   238→   238→> v\
   239→   239→> DENIAL TRIAGE\
   240→   240→> v\
   241→   241→> POLICY + EVIDENCE RESEARCH\
   242→   242→> v\
   243→   243→> APPEAL GENERATION\
   244→   244→> v\
   245→   245→> INDEPENDENT VERIFICATION\
   246→   246→> v\
   247→   247→> HUMAN APPROVAL\
   248→   248→> v\
   249→   249→> DEADLINE MANAGEMENT\
   250→   250→> v\
   251→   251→> OUTCOME RECORDED\
   252→   252→> v\
   253→   253→> MEASURED LEARNING\
   254→   254→> v\
   255→   255→> BETTER NEXT CASE
   256→   256→
   257→   257→This pivot directly attacks the three weaknesses the reviewer commentary
   258→   258→identifies as existential: domain credibility, the synthetic-data
   259→   259→criticism, and the HIPAA-perception problem. By making institutional
   260→   260→knowledge real (sourced from CMS and payer public material) while
   261→   261→keeping patient cases synthetic, the project can truthfully state that
   262→   262→its grounding evidence is authoritative and public, even though its
   263→   263→cases are privacy-safe by construction. The distinction is subtle but
   264→   264→decisive in a judge\'s mind: \"our data is synthetic\" is a confession;
   265→   265→\"our patient cases are synthetic by design, and the healthcare
   266→   266→knowledge grounding them is sourced from authoritative public payer and
   267→   267→government material\" is a credibility statement.

   269→   269→**3. Three Fatal Weaknesses of "Build-As-Written"**
   270→   270→
   271→   271→The reviewer\'s forensics on the existing blueprint converge on three
   272→   272→failure modes that, left uncorrected, would cap the project at a strong
   273→   273→track prize rather than the Grand Prize. Each is stated here as a
   274→   274→steel-manned objection so that the engineering plan in Parts IV and V
   275→   275→can be read as direct responses.
   276→   276→
   277→   277→**Weakness 1 --- The Outcome-Learning gimmick (severity: critical)**
   278→   278→
   279→   279→The blueprint\'s self-evolving moment is a procedural-evidence weight
   280→   280→changing from 0.42 to 0.48. Visually this is excellent, but a
   281→   281→sophisticated judge can ask, \"So you changed a number. How do you know
   282→   282→the system actually improved?\" Without an experimental answer, the most
   283→   283→agentic claim in the entire submission collapses into a UI animation.
   284→   284→The fix is to convert Outcome Learning from a performed weight-change
   285→   285→into a measured before/after evaluation across a held-out case set,
   286→   286→demonstrated live on two cases where the ranking of arguments visibly
   287→   287→changes because of recorded outcomes. This single change converts the
   288→   288→project from \"an agent that changes its weights\" into \"a system whose
   289→   289→behavior changed because of validated outcomes\" --- the latter is
   290→   290→genuinely agentic; the former is theater.
   291→   291→
   292→   292→**Weakness 2 --- Synthetic-only data leaves no credibility floor
   293→   293→(severity: high)**
   294→   294→
   295→   295→A demo built entirely on synthetic denial letters, synthetic medical
   296→   296→records, and a synthetic outcome corpus has no defensible answer to
   297→   297→\"where does your medical knowledge come from?\" The answer \"we
   298→   298→generated it\" is exactly the answer that triggers hallucination
   299→   299→concerns. The fix is to anchor the evidence corpus in authoritative
   300→   300→public sources --- CMS denial reason statements and eMDR codes, CMS
   301→   301→public-use claims files, CMS SynPUFs (which CMS explicitly created so
   302→   302→developers can work with realistic Medicare claims structures while
   303→   303→protecting beneficiary privacy), official Medicare appeals guidance, and
   304→   304→versioned, frozen payer medical-policy documents. CMS itself
   305→   305→distinguishes public-use files from identifiable data and states that
   306→   306→public-use files do not contain PHI or PII requiring a data-use
   307→   307→agreement, which gives the project a clean, citable data story.
   308→   308→
   309→   309→**Weakness 3 --- The \"Google product parade\" dilutes reliability
   310→   310→(severity: high)**
   311→   311→
   312→   312→The blueprint is committed to all seven Google Enterprise Agent Platform
   313→   313→(GEAP) components plus Gemma, Veo, Lyria, A2A, and MCP. The official
   314→   314→rules require only Gemini plus one Google agent framework plus one
   315→   315→Google Cloud infrastructure service; the GEAP components are recommended
   316→   316→for the Fleet track, not each individually mandatory. Shipping seven
   317→   317→labels at the cost of reliability produces a demo in which no single
   318→   318→integration is undeniably excellent and several are stubbed --- exactly
   319→   319→the failure pattern the blueprint\'s own kill-your-darlings philosophy
   320→   320→warns against. The fix is ruthless trimming: keep the core (ADK, Cloud
   321→   321→Run, Firestore, Cloud SQL pgvector, Pub/Sub) and the governance trio
   322→   322→(Model Armor, Agent Identity, Agent Observability); treat Memory Bank,
   323→   323→Gateway, Registry, Veo, Lyria, A2A, and MCP as conditional additions
   324→   324→that survive only if they demonstrably strengthen the narrative without
   325→   325→compromising the demo.

   411→   411→**5. The Triad Architecture (Evidence · Agents · Governance)**
   412→   412→
   413→   413→The judging architecture a judge should see at the end of the demo is a
   414→   414→triad: Evidence, Agents, and Governance, joined at a human-approval
   415→   415→vertex, leading to an appeal package, an outcome, and measured learning.
   416→   416→This is deliberately not a diagram of \"eight agents\" or a \"Google
   417→   417→stack\" --- those are implementation details that sit inside the Agents
   418→   418→vertex. The triad is the product; everything else is plumbing in service
   419→   419→of it. The discipline of drawing the architecture this way forces every
   420→   420→component to justify itself against one of the three vertices or the
   421→   421→outcome loop, and it makes the multi-agent necessity argument visual
   422→   422→rather than rhetorical.
   423→   423→
   424→   424→*Figure 5.1 --- The judging triad. The product is the loop, not the
   425→   425→agent count or the stack.*
   426→   426→
   427→   427→> DENIALDEFENDER\
   428→   428→> \|\
   429→   429→> +\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\--+\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\-\--+\
   430→   430→> \| \| \|\
   431→   431→> EVIDENCE AGENTS GOVERNANCE\
   432→   432→> \| \| \|\
   433→   433→> CMS / payer 8 specialists PHI Guard\
   434→   434→> public sources ADK workflow Model Armor\

   438→   438→> \|\

   452→   452→
   453→   453→Three architectural consequences follow from the triad. First, the
   454→   454→Evidence vertex is a first-class subsystem with its own storage,
   455→   455→versioning, and provenance --- not a vector index bolted onto the
   456→   456→agents. Second, the Agents vertex is internally a workflow orchestrated
   457→   457→by ADK, but externally it presents as a single decision-trace stream, so
   458→   458→judges never have to reason about agent topology to understand a case.
   459→   459→Third, the Governance vertex is co-equal with the other two: PHI Guard,
   460→   460→Model Armor, Agent Identity, and Agent Observability are not security
   461→   461→afterthoughts tacked onto a working pipeline --- they are the third
   462→   462→pillar of the product, and the PHI Guard in particular is a demo-grade
   463→   463→feature, not a backend detail.
   464→   464→
   465→   465→**Why a triad and not a stack**
   466→   466→
   467→   467→A stack diagram invites the judge to ask \"why so many boxes?\" A triad
   468→   468→invites the judge to ask \"is each pillar genuinely necessary?\" ---
   469→   469→which is the question the project can actually answer. Removing Evidence
   470→   470→breaks citation grounding; removing Agents breaks the workflow and the
   471→   471→multi-agent necessity argument; removing Governance breaks the
   472→   472→enterprise-grade and production-readiness criteria. The triad is
   473→   473→therefore the minimum architecture that satisfies all three judging
   474→   474→dimensions simultaneously, which is why it is the headline diagram
   475→   475→rather than the agent graph.
   476→   476→
   477→   477→**6. Agent Architecture --- Eight Genuinely Necessary Specialists**
   478→   478→
   479→   479→Eight specialized agents implement the workflow. The headline is not
   480→   480→\"eight agents\" --- it is \"a verified appeal workflow that learns from
   481→   481→outcomes, implemented by eight specialists.\" A sophisticated judge does
   482→   482→not care whether the count is 8, 12, or 34; they care why these
   483→   483→boundaries are necessary. The table below is the one-screen architecture
   484→   484→justification: each agent, its single responsibility, and what
   485→   485→measurably breaks if it is removed. This table is also the basis of the
   486→   486→ablation experiment in Section 7.

   489→   489→Section 7 turns it into a measured ablation.*

   550→   550→**7. Necessity Justification & the Agent-Ablation Experiment**

   692→   692→**9. Provenance & Citation Architecture**
   693→   693→
   694→   694→Provenance is elevated from a backend detail to a core product feature.
   695→   695→Every important claim in an appeal carries a provenance card the judge
   696→   696→can open with one click, which directly attacks the hallucination
   697→   697→concern that sinks most LLM-in-healthcare submissions. The provenance
   698→   698→record is a structured object, not a free-text citation, so it is
   699→   699→machine-checkable by the Quality Review agent and human-checkable by the
   700→   700→judge in seconds. This is the single most cost-effective credibility
   701→   701→upgrade in the entire plan: it costs roughly one day of engineering (Day
   702→   702→5) and lifts the Innovation and Architecture scores simultaneously.
   703→   703→
   704→   704→*Figure 9.1 --- The provenance card. Every citation in an appeal
   705→   705→resolves to one of these; the judge clicks "Why did the agent say this?"
   706→   706→and gets this.*
   707→   707→
   708→   708→> SOURCE CMS / PAYER\
   709→   709→> DOCUMENT Medical Policy X\
   710→   710→> SECTION 4.2\
   711→   711→> EFFECTIVE DATE 2026-01-01\
   712→   712→> RETRIEVED 2026-08-15\
   713→   713→> PROVENANCE AUTHORITATIVE / PUBLIC\
   714→   714→> STATUS VERIFIED\
   715→   715→> CONTENT HASH sha256:9f2c\...e1a
   716→   716→
   717→   717→Three properties make the provenance system credible rather than
   718→   718→decorative. First, every evidence document is content-hashed at
   719→   719→ingestion, so the version used at appeal time is the version on disk ---
   720→   720→a judge can verify the hash. Second, effective dates are mandatory; an
   721→   721→appeal never cites a clause whose effective date has lapsed, because the
   722→   722→Compliance agent rejects it. Third, the provenance status field is one
   723→   723→of a controlled set (verified, retrieved, model-generated, synthetic),
   724→   724→so the UI can color-code trust and the Quality Review agent can refuse
   725→   725→to pass a draft whose citations are not all verified or retrieved. This
   726→   726→is what makes the "5 citations checked → 5 verified" demo moment
   727→   727→truthful rather than aspirational.

   729→   729→**10. Security & Governance Architecture --- PHI Guard**
   730→   730→
   731→   731→The governance vertex of the triad is built from four components: PHI
   732→   732→Guard, Model Armor, Agent Identity, and Agent Observability. PHI Guard
   733→   733→is the demo-grade feature; the other three are the enterprise-governance
   734→   734→proof. PHI Guard sits in front of the agent fleet and classifies every
   735→   735→upload before any model invocation: synthetic or public content is
   736→   736→allowed through; potential PHI is blocked and the block is logged. The
   737→   737→decisive property is that a block means no model invocation occurred ---
   738→   738→which is both a real security guarantee and an excellent
   739→   739→enterprise-security demo moment.
   740→   740→
   741→   741→*Figure 10.1 --- PHI Guard. A block guarantees zero model invocation,
   742→   742→which is the security wow moment.*
   743→   743→
   744→   744→> Upload\

   746→   746→> v\
   747→   747→> PHI Guard (classifier: is this potential PHI?)\
   748→   748→> +\-- Synthetic / Public \--\> ALLOW \--\> agent fleet\
   749→   749→> +\-- Potential PHI \--\> BLOCK \--\> no model invocation, logged

   752→   752→the banner reads "No PHI detected --- processing allowed." The second
   753→   753→upload is a deliberately constructed sensitive document, and the banner
   754→   754→reads "Potential PHI detected --- processing blocked. No model
   755→   755→invocation occurred." That contrast is the security wow and it is
   756→   756→truthful because the classifier runs before any agent. Model Armor is

   761→   761→outcomes, and Agent Observability sinks the decision-trace Pub/Sub
   762→   762→stream into a queryable store so every case is auditable end-to-end.

   916→   916→**13. The Outcome-Learning Experiment (Measured, Not Performed)**
   917→   917→
   918→   918→This is the most important technical recommendation in the entire
   919→   919→document and the single biggest strategic change from the blueprint as
   920→   920→written. Outcome Learning must be converted from a performed
   921→   921→weight-change animation into a measured before/after evaluation
   922→   922→demonstrated live across two cases. The blueprint\'s target of at least
   923→   923→20% improvement in top-3 retrieval is kept, but it must be measured
   924→   924→before it is claimed. A judge can always ask "how do you know the system
   925→   925→improved?" --- the only defensible answer is an experiment.
   926→   926→
   927→   927→**Experimental design**
   928→   928→
   929→   929→The learning corpus is built from three legitimate sources: public
   930→   930→authoritative evidence, public appeal-decision material, and synthetic
   931→   931→controlled cases --- not "50 fake wins." CMS\'s public appeals materials
   932→   932→demonstrate that the appeal process is multi-stage and outcome-oriented,
   933→   933→which gives the corpus a defensible outcome signal. The evaluation
   934→   934→harness runs as follows: fifty synthetic training outcomes are ingested;
   935→   935→weights are updated; the same ten held-out cases are scored before and
   936→   936→after. Five metrics are measured --- top-1 retrieval accuracy, top-3
   937→   937→retrieval accuracy, citation grounding, argument selection, and appeal
   938→   938→quality. The result is reported whether or not it flatters the system,
   939→   939→because an honest negative result is more credible than an inflated
   940→   940→positive one.
   941→   941→
   942→   942→*Figure 13.1 --- Before/after evaluation. The same ten cases, scored
   943→   943→twice; the delta is the agentic claim.*
   944→   944→
   945→   945→> BEFORE LEARNING\
   946→   946→> -\> retrieve top-3 arguments on 10 held-out cases\
   947→   947→> -\> score (top-1 acc, top-3 acc, grounding, argument, quality)\
   948→   948→> -\> snapshot metrics\
   949→   949→> \
   950→   950→> THEN\
   951→   951→> -\> ingest 50 outcome records (public + synthetic)\
   952→   952→> -\> update procedural-evidence weights\
   953→   953→> \
   954→   954→> AFTER LEARNING\
   955→   955→> -\> run same 10 held-out cases\
   956→   956→> -\> score again\
   957→   957→> -\> diff metrics (target: top-3 retrieval +\>=20%)
   958→   958→
   959→   959→**The two-case live demonstration**
   960→   960→
   961→   961→The measured experiment is the technical artifact; the two-case live
   962→   962→demonstration is the demo artifact. Case 1 runs to a LOSS, the system
   963→   963→updates procedural evidence, and Case 2 --- same payer, related denial
   964→   964→--- runs with a visibly different argument ranking, which the agent
   965→   965→explains: "This ranking changed because two previous validated outcomes
   966→   966→favored Argument C." That is actual observable learning. A judge
   967→   967→understands it instantly, and it is vastly stronger than a green
   968→   968→"+0.06." The before/after metric ("Top-3 retrieval accuracy: 60% → 80%")
   969→   969→is shown only if it was actually measured; an unmeasured number is never
   970→   970→displayed.
   971→   971→
   972→   972→**14. Decision Trace --- Not "Agent Thoughts"**
   973→   973→
   974→   974→The blueprint repeatedly says "every agent\'s thoughts visible." That
   975→   975→phrasing is technically and conceptually dangerous. Exposing private
   976→   976→chain-of-thought invites the judge to evaluate the reasoning process
   977→   977→rather than the decision, and it pretends to expose hidden reasoning
   978→   978→that the model does not actually expose faithfully. The replacement is a
   979→   979→structured decision trace: discrete, named, verifiable events emitted by
   980→   980→each agent as it completes a step. This gives judges transparency
   981→   981→without pretending to expose hidden reasoning, and it is safer
   982→   982→conceptually because the trace is a record of decisions, not a
   983→   983→confession of internal monologue.
   984→   984→
   985→   985→*Figure 14.1 --- Decision trace. Structured, named, verifiable events;
   986→   986→not chain-of-thought.*
   987→   987→
   988→   988→> Triage\
   989→   989→> \[x\] payer identified\
   990→   990→> \[x\] denial type classified\
   991→   991→> \[x\] deadline extracted\
   992→   992→> \
   993→   993→> Policy Research\
   994→   994→> \[x\] 3 sources retrieved\
   995→   995→> \[x\] clause UNH-MN-014 selected\
   996→   996→> \
   997→   997→> Evidence\
   998→   998→> \[x\] 4 supporting records found\
   999→   999→> \
  1000→  1000→> Quality Review\
  1001→  1001→> \[x\] 5/5 citations verified\
  1002→  1002→> \[x\] 0 unsupported claims
  1003→  1003→
  1004→  1004→The decision trace is emitted to a Pub/Sub topic by each agent at step
  1005→  1005→completion, persisted to Firestore as part of the case record, and
  1006→  1006→rendered in the UI as a live event stream during the demo. Because each
  1007→  1007→event is a structured object (agent, step, status, evidence references,
  1008→  1008→timestamp), the Quality Review agent can read the trace to verify that
  1009→  1009→every citation it later checks was actually retrieved by Policy Research
  1010→  1010→--- which closes the loop between trace and verification. This is what
  1011→  1011→makes the trace an audit artifact rather than a decorative animation.
  1012→  1012→
  1013→  1013→**15. Adversarial Quality Review**

  1150→  1150→**19. Repository & Service Topology**
  1151→  1151→
  1152→  1152→The repository is a monorepo with a small number of deployable Cloud Run
  1153→  1153→services. A monorepo is chosen over a multi-repo split because the team
  1154→  1154→is small (3--4 engineers), the build is short (14 days), and the cost of
  1155→  1155→cross-repo coordination exceeds the cost of a slightly larger single
  1156→  1156→repo. Each Cloud Run service has a single responsibility and scales
  1157→  1157→independently, which satisfies the production-readiness criterion
  1158→  1158→without introducing Kubernetes --- the blueprint\'s kill of Kubernetes
  1159→  1159→is accepted.

  1162→  1162→app, two shared packages.*

  1168→  1168→> ingest/ -\> Cloud Run (evidence corpus loader, hasher, versioner)\

  1190→  1190→agents service provably passed the guard. The eval service is the
  1191→  1191→experimental backbone described in Section 21.

  1239→  1239→**21. The Evaluation Harness --- Experimental Backbone**
  1240→  1240→
  1241→  1241→The evaluation harness is the most important engineering artifact in the
  1242→  1242→submission, because it produces the two strongest technical artifacts
  1243→  1243→--- the agent ablation (Section 7) and the outcome-learning before/after
  1244→  1244→(Section 13) --- from a single set of held-out cases. It is built as the
  1245→  1245→eval Cloud Run service and is run nightly so that a regression in any
  1246→  1246→metric is visible before it reaches the demo. The harness is
  1247→  1247→deterministic: the same case set and the same model configuration
  1248→  1248→produce the same scores, so before/after deltas are attributable to the
  1249→  1249→learning update rather than to model non-determinism. Temperature is
  1250→  1250→pinned to zero for evaluation runs; only the live demo uses a slightly
  1251→  1251→higher temperature for narrative quality.
  1252→  1252→
  1253→  1253→**Held-out case set**
  1254→  1254→
  1255→  1255→Ten held-out cases are reserved and never used for weight updates. They
  1256→  1256→are constructed to span the denial categories the triage classifier must
  1257→  1257→distinguish (medical necessity, coding, prior authorization,
  1258→  1258→experimental, out-of-network, other) and to include at least one case
  1259→  1259→per primary payer in the corpus. The same ten cases are scored before
  1260→  1260→learning, after learning, and across each ablation topology, which means
  1261→  1261→one harness run produces the evidence for both killer artifacts. The
  1262→  1262→case set is checked into the repo under data/cases/held_out/ and is
  1263→  1263→versioned; any change to it is a commit that must be justified, because
  1264→  1264→silently altering the held-out set is the easiest way to invalidate
  1265→  1265→every metric in the submission.

  1267→  1267→**22. Reliability & Demo-Failure Plan**
  1268→  1268→
  1269→  1269→A demo that fails live is a submission that fails. The reliability plan
  1270→  1270→assumes the live demo will encounter at least one degraded condition and
  1271→  1271→engineers for it. Three paths guarantee the demo can always complete: a
  1272→  1272→live path (the real system), a cached-rollback path (pre-recorded
  1273→  1273→decision traces and drafts for the exact demo cases, replayed if latency
  1274→  1274→or an error is detected), and a static-fallback path (a narrated
  1275→  1275→walkthrough of the same artifacts if the network is unavailable). The
  1276→  1276→operator can switch paths in under five seconds without the audience
  1277→  1277→noticing, because all three render the same decision-trace and
  1278→  1278→provenance UI.

  1859→  1859→**36. Counterarguments & Responses (Steelmanned)**
  1860→  1860→
  1861→  1861→A strong submission pre-empts its own objections. Five counterarguments
  1862→  1862→are steelmanned below --- each is made as strong as possible before the
  1863→  1863→response is given, so that the responses survive a skeptical judge
  1864→  1864→rather than a friendly one. The pattern for each is: state the objection
  1865→  1865→at full strength, concede what is true, define the limits, and explain
  1866→  1866→why the doctrine in Part I still holds.
  1867→  1867→
  1868→  1868→**Counterargument 1 --- Synthetic cases mean the system is unproven on
  1869→  1869→real denials.**
  1870→  1870→
  1871→  1871→Steel-man: a demo on synthetic data does not prove the system works on
  1872→  1872→real payer letters, so the operational-utility claim is unsupported.
  1873→  1873→Concession: the cases are indeed synthetic, and the system is not
  1874→  1874→claimed to be production-validated. Limit and response: the
  1875→  1875→institutional knowledge is not synthetic --- it is sourced from CMS and
  1876→  1876→payer public material, versioned and hashed, with provenance cards the
  1877→  1877→judge can verify. The Triage agent classifies against CMS\'s published
  1878→  1878→denial-reason statements, not free text. The domain-validation panel
  1879→  1879→records a specialist\'s concrete changes. So the claim is scoped:
  1880→  1880→evidence-backed appeal draft on synthetic cases grounded in real public
  1881→  1881→evidence, not proven on real denials. The doctrine holds because the
  1882→  1882→scope is explicit and defensible, not overclaimed.
  1883→  1883→
  1884→  1884→**Counterargument 2 --- Eight agents is over-engineered; three would
  1885→  1885→do.**
  1886→  1886→
  1887→  1887→Steel-man: most denial-appeal work is triage, draft, review, so eight
  1888→  1888→agents is theater. Concession: a three-agent pipeline can produce a
  1889→  1889→draft. Limit and response: the ablation table (Section 7) is measured,
  1890→  1890→not asserted, and is designed to show that each removed agent
  1891→  1891→reintroduces a measurable failure --- collapsing Policy Research and
  1892→  1892→Evidence Assembly destroys the citation/clinical-evidence distinction;
  1893→  1893→removing Quality Review reintroduces unsupported claims the compliance
  1894→  1894→story cannot tolerate. The count is justified by measurement; if the
  1895→  1895→ablation does not show a marginal lift per agent, the doctrine requires
  1896→  1896→reporting the honest result, and the agent is a candidate for
  1897→  1897→Kill-Your-Darlings. The doctrine holds because the necessity argument is
  1898→  1898→empirical, not rhetorical.
  1899→  1899→
  1900→  1900→**Counterargument 3 --- The outcome-learning result could be a measured
  1901→  1901→negative.**
  1902→  1902→
  1903→  1903→Steel-man: if fifty outcomes do not move top-3 retrieval by 20 percent,
  1904→  1904→the headline agentic claim fails. Concession: this is a real risk and
  1905→  1905→the experiment might not flatter the system. Limit and response:
  1906→  1906→Principle 5 (honest negative results are acceptable) is explicit that a
  1907→  1907→measured non-improvement is reported, not hidden --- and a credible
  1908→  1908→negative result with a sound experiment is far stronger than an inflated
  1909→  1909→positive claim a judge can debunk in thirty seconds. Even a modest
  1910→  1910→positive delta, honestly measured and shown live across two cases, is a
  1911→  1911→stronger agentic artifact than any animation. The doctrine holds because
  1912→  1912→credibility is the objective, not a specific number.
  1913→  1913→
  1914→  1914→**Counterargument 4 --- The Google stack is still too broad; something

  1916→  1916→
