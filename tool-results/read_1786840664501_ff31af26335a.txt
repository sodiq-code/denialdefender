    25→# 1. Executive Verdict
    26→
    27→DenialDefender is the Grand Prize thesis for the All Things Agentic Hackathon: an 8-agent ADK 2.0 fleet that turns medical insurance claim denials into winning appeal letters — autonomously parsing denial letters, retrieving payer policy and patient-protection law, assembling medical-necessity evidence, drafting citation-backed appeals, and coordinating at clinic scale for medical billing specialists. It is filed under The Fortified Enterprise Fleet track because the multi-agent fleet is the architectural core, but it is engineered to score maximally on all three track rubrics so it competes for the $50,000 Grand Prize rather than just the $20,000 Track Prize. It wins because it is the only candidate in our 50-concept shortlist that simultaneously satisfies all three hidden mandates — BYOF (visceral personal pain: 1 in 7 insured Americans face claim denials annually [D-1]), Unlikely Hero (medical billing specialists, NOT executives or developers [D-2]), and Evolving Knowledge (payer policies, regulations, and appeal success patterns mutate continuously [A-7]) — while using every mandated Google product deeply (Gemini 3.5 Flash 1M context, ADK 2.0 Collaborative workflows, all 7 GEAP components, Cloud Run + Firestore + Cloud SQL pgvector + Pub/Sub, plus all 3 bonus models Gemma/Veo/Lyria each doing a distinct real job). The memorability sentence is unforgettable on first hearing: "This is the project that fights your insurance company for you when they deny your claim — and then teaches every other patient how to win theirs." It echoes Google's own "agentic Gemini era" narrative [B-3] because it visibly embodies the transition from chatbot to autonomous action-taking agent. The wow moment — a real (de-identified) denial letter dropped into the UI producing a complete citation-backed appeal letter in under 90 seconds, with every agent's reasoning visible in real time — is precisely the "live external interaction + multi-output artifact generation" pattern that won the SalesShortcut Grand Prize (real phone call) and the Unravel Fivetran-track first prize (clinician alert + family letter + ClinVar submission in one screen) [C-1, C-2].    433→## Synthesis
   434→
   435→The explicit rubric and implicit dynamics reinforce each other on one axis: the demo's wow moment must be unmistakable, live, and unedited. They diverge on a second axis: the rubric weights Innovation at 40% (problem severity + autonomy + Unlikely Hero mandate), while implicit dynamics weight Polish, Memorability, and Demo Reliability effectively at CRITICAL. The implication is that DenialDefender must optimize for both — pick a problem with maximum visceral Innovation score AND rehearse the demo until it cannot fail. The 4-minute constraint is unforgiving: every second must serve either the problem, the wow, or the tech-stack proof. We allocate seconds in the demo script (Section 11 of the full blueprint) to satisfy both layers simultaneously.
